What I built

Five modules, one go-to-market plan

Each module added one decision. Together they set who Roamly Groups is for, how it wins and how it makes money.

Module 1
GTM strategy
Target segment

Friend-group organisers: existing Roamly travellers planning a celebration for 4–12 friends. They're the people behind the workarounds in Roamly's support data, they front the bill and chase friends for money, and every group they book brings in friends who don't use Roamly yet.

GTM motion

Hybrid: product-led first, community-led second. The group invite is the growth loop (every booking reaches 3–11 people new to Roamly), and community is how Roamly already grows: shared plans, photos and reviews become content Roamly didn't have to make

Primary goal

Acquire: net-new travellers who come in through the friends each organiser invites. Primary metric: the share of invited friends who make their own booking within 90 days

Module 2
Battlecard and strategic bet
Primary competitor

Airbnb Experiences: the same promise, already in the organiser's hand, private groups of up to 30, but one person pays for everyone (it retired Group Payments)

Strategic bet

Owning group experiences for existing Roamly travellers who organise celebrations for 4–12 friends: vetted, group-ready hosts with pay-your-share and one shared plan built in, launched to our own base first and spreading through the friends they invite

Deprioritizing

Paid acquisition of organisers outside Roamly, and big parties of 13–30 (weddings, offsites), where Airbnb's private groups already win. Both come back once the loop works with our own base

Module 3
Positioning
Target audience

The friend who always ends up organising: an existing Roamly traveller planning a birthday, hen or stag weekend or reunion for 4–12 friends, many of whom have never used Roamly

Positioning statement

Roamly Groups lets the friend who always ends up organising book a vetted local experience for the whole group, with hosts ready for groups, every friend paying their own share and one plan everyone can see. It's built for celebrations planned around what you'll do together, not where you'll sleep, so the organiser gets to enjoy the day instead of fronting the bill and chasing friends for money.

Module 4
Messaging pillars
From/To shift

From: one person finds the plan, pays for everyone, chases each friend for their share and resends the plan in the group chat.

To: everyone pays their own part before it's booked, everyone sees the plan, and the organiser is a guest at their own celebration

Audience 1 pillar

The organiser · Value prop: plan the celebration, not the admin.

Capabilities: one invite link, each friend pays their share before booking, one shared plan, group-ready vetted hosts.

Evidence: rated among the best in its category, repeat bookings at 3× the industry average, 12,000+ vetted experts in 40 cities;

Groups-specific proof comes from launch.

Audience 2, the invited friend · Say yes to the plan and pay just your part, in a couple of taps

Copy across three surfaces

In-app notification: Booking for the whole group? Send one link and everyone pays their own share. You don't cover anyone.

Email subject line: Planning the birthday? This time, you don't pay for everyone.

Social post: The friend who books the hen weekend shouldn't also be the one chasing eight people for money. Send one link, and everyone pays their own share

Module 5
Pricing recommendation
Goal and model

Enter a new market, with transaction-based pricing: each friend pays their own share of the experience plus a small fee, a percentage of that share with a cap. No subscription, because groups book a few times a year, not every month

Tiers

Split: 4–6 friends, one experience, ~3% of each share (max €3).

Celebration ★: 4–12 friends, a group-ready host, and drop-outs don't fall on the organiser, ~5% (max €6).

Celebration Plus: a multi-experience day with a private host, ~8% (max €12).

Fees are placeholders to test; a percentage keeps the fee in proportion to the booking

Assumption to test

Invited friends who don't know Roamly will pay their share plus the fee without dropping out. I'd test it with Van Westendorp for the fee range, plus a one-city pilot that tracks real payment completion

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

pricing. The first fees were flat per person, and you spotted that they ignored the booking amount (a flat €9 would have been 45% of a €20 share), so they became a capped percentage. Or: there is no Groups data anywhere, so every number had to stay a labelled assumption

Key learnings

never write a pillar or a price without evidence, and label what's assumed. Or: each module's decision constrains the next one (the M2 bet set the M5 goal)

Aha moment

in M2, finding that Airbnb built group payments in 2017 and then retired them. Split payment alone couldn't be the headline, so the value proposition moved to vetted, group-ready experiences. Or: the M3 blind read, when your partner got the audience but missed "the organiser gets to enjoy the day"
