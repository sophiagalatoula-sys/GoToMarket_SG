## 1. Primary competitor
- **Competitor:** Airbnb Experiences
- **Core offering:** Bookable local experiences (tours, workshops, food, outdoor activities) inside the Airbnb app, alongside stays and the new Services. Experiences can be booked publicly or as a **private group of up to 30 people**. Relaunched in May 2025 after a two-year pause, with a new top tier, "Airbnb Originals".
- **How they position themselves:** Local experiences hosted by interesting locals, with "Originals" hosted by "the world's most interesting people doing interesting things in amazing places". Experiences are made part of the trip you already booked on Airbnb, and leadership has said they need to be cheaper and more social to win younger travellers.
- **Primary audience:** Travellers already using Airbnb for their stay, with a push toward younger travellers.
- **Known pricing or packaging:** Priced per guest, roughly $20 to $200+ (reported for the relaunch). For a private group, the host sets a minimum price: the group pays the minimum, or the per-guest total if that is higher (Airbnb's example: $100 minimum at $20 a guest covers up to 5 people, then $20 per extra guest). **Payment:** a single payer. Airbnb launched Group Payments (split payments) in 2017 and later retired it; new reservations can't be split between guests.
- **Why they're the biggest threat:** They make the same promise as Roamly (local experiences with local hosts) to the same organizer, and they are already in the organizer's hand: the friend group often books the stay on Airbnb, and Experiences appear in the same app. They can take a group booking of up to 30 people today, and their brand is one every invited friend already trusts. They are a **primary** competitor: in the same decision, at the moment the organizer is choosing.

### Other competitors considered
- **GetYourGuide / Viator:** mass-market tour and activity marketplaces with the widest inventory and verified reviews. They don't build for groups: no native split payment for private group tours found, and large groups are routed to separate forms. *Secondary competitor:* solves part of the problem, not built for the organizer.
- **Last Night of Freedom** (UK; the one I hadn't considered): a hen and stag party organiser selling packaged weekends in UK and European destinations (Eastern Europe, Western Europe, Spain & Portugal). **It already lets each group member pay their own share:** one small deposit for the whole group (£35), then every member gets their own login to pay online. Others in Europe work the same way (Funktion Events, 73 UK and European destinations; GoHen). It's strong on the organizer's money pain for exactly our launch focus, celebration trips. It's weaker on the experience itself: packaged party activities rather than curated, vetted local experts, aimed mainly at British groups. *Secondary, worth watching:* it proves "everyone pays their own share" is already table stakes for celebration trips in Europe.
- **Status quo:** the organizer books on any marketplace with their own card, plans in a WhatsApp group, and tracks and chases each friend's share through Splitwise or Venmo. Free and familiar, and the workaround Roamly's own support data already shows.


## 2. Battlecard

#### Know the Landscape
- **Competitor overview:** Airbnb's experiences business, relaunched in May 2025 inside the main Airbnb app next to stays and Services. A stays company adding experiences, with a top tier ("Originals") that is exclusive to Airbnb.
- **Key offerings:** public and private-group experiences (private groups up to 30); Originals; the experience appears in the same app and account as the stay; guests can message other attendees before booking.
- **Pricing:** per guest, ~$20–$200+; private groups pay the host's minimum or the per-guest total, whichever is higher; **one payer per booking** (Group Payments retired).

#### Understand the Battlefield
- **Key strengths:**
  - Distribution and default: hundreds of millions of travellers are already in the app, and the organizer may already be booking the stay there.
  - Brand trust: every invited friend knows Airbnb, so paying through it feels safe.
  - One place for the whole trip: stay + experience, one account.
  - Capacity for bigger groups: private bookings up to 30.
  - A strategy pushing toward cheaper, more social experiences.
- **Key weaknesses:**
  - **The organizer fronts the whole bill.** There's no way to split payment for an experience booking, so the organizer collects from friends through other apps.
  - **No shared group plan:** each friend relies on the organizer's screenshots and messages.
  - **Experiences are a side business** next to stays. They were paused for two years, and the relaunch dropped the exclusivity requirement for regular Experiences, so quality and vetting depend on the host.
  - **Private-group availability is the host's choice**, so a group can't assume an experience will take them.

#### Win the Deal
- **Why you win:**
  - **Everyone pays their own share**, natively, for experiences. Nobody fronts the bill or chases friends.
  - **One shared itinerary** the whole group sees.
  - **Vetted local experts** consistently rated best in category, with repeat bookings at 3× the industry average. Experiences are Roamly's whole business, not an add-on to stays.
  - **Group-ready supply:** 12,000+ vetted hosts across 40 cities, ready to support group bookings.
  - *Defensibility, honestly:* split payment on its own can be copied (Airbnb has built it before). What is harder to copy is **curated hosts set up for groups**, plus what Roamly learns about how groups book. That's where the moat has to be built.
- **Why they win:**
  - **They own the moment.** If the organizer is already booking the stay on Airbnb, the experience is one tap away. Roamly has to be found separately, and today most organizers won't look.
  - **Invitees trust Airbnb, not Roamly.** Our Acquire goal relies on invited friends paying through a brand many of them have never heard of.
  - **They take the big parties:** groups of 13–30 (weddings, offsites, big birthdays) have a home on Airbnb, and Roamly's launch focus stops at 12.
  - **They can close our gap fast.** Airbnb had group payments in 2017 and could bring them back. If they do, our clearest functional edge disappears.
  - **Price pressure:** leadership has said experiences need to get cheaper.
- **Why you (vs doing nothing):** today the organizer pays the full bill on their own card, then spends the trip chasing money and resending plans. With Roamly Groups the group books a top-rated local experience in one go, everyone pays their own share, and the booking doesn't collapse when someone drops out. That beats the free workaround because it removes the organizer's money risk, not just the admin.

#### Handle the Hard Moments
- **Quick dismiss:** "Airbnb is built for where you sleep. Roamly is built for what your group does together, and everyone pays their own share."
- **Landmine questions:**
  - "When you book an experience for eight friends on Airbnb, who pays, and how do you get the money back?"
  - "What happens to the booking if two people drop out the week before?"
  - "Can everyone in the group see the same plan for the day?"
  - "How was this host vetted, and does the experience actually take private groups?"
- **Objection handling:**
  - *"We already booked the stay on Airbnb."* Keep it. Roamly is for the part of the trip you'll remember, and it doesn't replace the stay. Everyone pays their own share, so you aren't fronting another bill.
  - *"Airbnb has more choice and might be cheaper."* Compare the price per person you actually pay, not the headline: on Airbnb the organizer carries the whole bill and any unpaid shares. Roamly's hosts are vetted and rated best in category.
  - *"My friends have never heard of Roamly."* They don't need an account to see the plan. They pay their share in a couple of taps, and the experience itself is the reason Roamly's repeat rate is 3× the industry average.
  - *"Splitwise already handles the money."* Splitwise tracks who owes what; you still pay first and chase later. Roamly collects each share before the booking is confirmed.
  - *"Airbnb will just add split payments."* They might. Split payment isn't the whole product: group-ready, vetted hosts and a plan the whole group shares are what Roamly is built around.

## 4. Strategic bet

### Data
- **Who Roamly Groups is for:** **I'm doubling down on friend-group organizers**, sharpened by the battlecard: **existing Roamly travellers who organize a celebration trip or shared experience for 4–12 friends.** They already trust Roamly, they are the users behind the workarounds in our data, and they're the organizers Airbnb doesn't own yet.
- **The one thing worth choosing it for:** It's the only place a group can book a vetted, top-rated local experience together with everyone paying their own share. On Airbnb the organizer fronts the whole bill.
- **The biggest threat to that position:** Airbnb owns the moment the organizer plans the trip (the stay is already in its app) and could rebuild group payments. Our functional edge can be copied; our reach is far smaller.

*The three signals, ranked:* (1) Airbnb has the reach and the default but has retired split payments and has no group plan. (2) The status quo (one card + WhatsApp + Splitwise) is free and familiar, so Roamly must win when the pain peaks, the moment the organizer is about to front the bill. (3) Roamly's own data shows the demand is already on Roamly, among existing users.

### Insight
The most surprising finding is that **Airbnb built split payments and then retired them.** For a stays company, group payment for experiences is a side feature it can drop; for Roamly, groups are the product. That changes how I see the market: the fight isn't feature against feature, it's **who the organizer thinks of when they plan what the group will do.** Airbnb wins by being there already. To take that buyer, Roamly Groups has to reach the organizer before they default to Airbnb, starting with the travellers who already trust Roamly, and make group-readiness (hosts set up for groups, payment and plan built in) something Airbnb's stay-first product doesn't have.

### Bet
- **I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by:** owning group experiences for existing Roamly travellers who organize celebration trips for 4–12 friends: group-ready, vetted hosts with per-person payment and one shared plan built in, launched first to our own base, where the workaround already happens, and spreading through the friends they invite.
- **To make this bet, I am deprioritizing:** paid acquisition of organizers who don't use Roamly yet, and large parties of 13–30 (weddings, corporate offsites, big celebrations), where Airbnb's private groups already win. Both come back once the loop works with our own base.
