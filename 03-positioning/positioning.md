## 1. Positioning framework

- **For (target audience):** The friend who always ends up organising: an existing Roamly traveller planning a celebration (a birthday, a hen or stag weekend, a reunion) for a group of 4–12 friends. They choose and book the experience, and their friends agree and pay. Many of those friends have never used Roamly.
- **Who want / need:** "I want to book something we'll all remember, without paying for everyone up front, chasing eight people for their share, and resending the plan every time someone asks." *(Written in the organiser's voice to describe the pattern Roamly's support team reports: group bookings across separate accounts, payments split by hand, availability synced across people. It is not a customer quote.)*
- **We offer:** Roamly Groups: a way to book one of Roamly's vetted local experiences (a food tour, a workshop, a day trip, an outdoor adventure) for your whole group in one place. You send your friends one link, each friend pays their own share, and everyone sees the same plan.
- **Because of (value proposition):** The organiser gets to enjoy the celebration instead of being the group's admin and debt collector. The group books something genuinely good together, and the booking no longer rests on one person's card or one person's inbox.
- **Despite (barriers):** Most of the organiser's friends have never heard of Roamly. Asking them to pay their share through an unfamiliar brand feels riskier than paying through Airbnb, which they all know and trust. Roamly has to earn each friend's trust at the moment they pay, and if it doesn't, the organiser looks bad in front of their friends.
- **Better than (differentiation):** Better than Airbnb Experiences for this organiser, because Airbnb is built around where you sleep and Roamly is built around what you do. On Airbnb one person pays for the whole experience booking (its group payments feature was retired), and whether a host takes private groups is the host's choice. Roamly's whole business is curated experiences: consistently rated among the best in its category, with repeat bookings at three times the industry average, and 12,000+ vetted hosts ready to support group bookings. Groups builds pay-your-share and a shared plan into those experiences.


## 2. Positioning statement

### First draft
Roamly Groups lets the friend who plans the celebration book a top-rated local experience for the whole group in one place, with every friend paying their own share. You get a day everyone remembers, without fronting the bill or chasing anyone for money.

### Pressure test
- **Test I ran:** B · Competitor discomfort (a product leader at Airbnb Experiences reads the statement: could Airbnb use it word for word?)
- **What it revealed:** Mostly, yes. "Book a top-rated local experience for the whole group in one place" is something Airbnb already says: it takes private groups of up to 30, and Originals is sold as its top tier. "Every friend paying their own share" is the only part Airbnb can't claim today, and it's a feature Airbnb has built before and could bring back, so it can't carry the position on its own. "A day everyone remembers" could sit on any travel homepage. Two mistakes from the course showed up: leaning on a feature (split payment) as the differentiation, and a claim that's true but not ownable ("top-rated"). What Airbnb can't say is that it is built around the experience rather than the stay, and that its hosts are set up for groups. The final statement leads with that, and keeps pay-your-share as the proof.

### Final statement
Roamly Groups lets the friend who always ends up organising book a vetted local experience for the whole group, with hosts ready for groups, every friend paying their own share and one plan everyone can see. It's built for celebrations planned around what you'll do together, not where you'll sleep, so the organiser gets to enjoy the day instead of fronting the bill and chasing friends for money.
