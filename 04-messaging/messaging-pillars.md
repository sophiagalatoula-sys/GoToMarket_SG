## 1. Fro/To market shift

- **From, life before:** A group celebration runs on one person. They find something to do, pay for everyone on their own card, chase each friend for their share, and resend the plan in the group chat every time someone asks. The group settles for whatever is easiest to book, and the organiser spends the celebration as the group's admin and debt collector.
- **To, life after:** Everyone commits to the plan by paying their own part before it's booked. The group chooses something worth remembering, everyone can see the plan, and the organiser gets to be a guest at their own celebration.


## 2. Audiences

- **Number of audiences that need their own message:** 2
- **Why you picked this number:** The person who books and the people who pay are different people, the course's "role or buying position" signal. The organiser chooses the experience and wants to stop carrying the money and the admin. The invited friend didn't choose anything, may never have heard of Roamly, and is being asked to pay. One message to the organiser would miss the friend completely, and the friend is the one the launch depends on: the goal is to acquire new travellers through the friends each organiser invites, and the biggest barrier in the positioning is that those friends don't know Roamly.


## 3. Messaging pillars

### Audience 1: The organiser (an existing Roamly traveller planning a celebration for 4–12 friends)
- **Value prop:** Plan the celebration, not the admin. Book something the whole group will remember, and stop paying for everyone and chasing friends for money.
- **Capabilities:**
  - Book one of Roamly's vetted local experiences for the whole group in one place.
  - Send friends a single invite link.
  - Each friend pays their own share before the booking is confirmed.
  - One shared plan the whole group can see.
  - Hosts ready to support group bookings.
- **Evidence:**
  - Roamly is consistently rated among the best in its category.
  - Repeat booking rates run at three times the industry average.
  - 12,000+ vetted local experts across 40 cities.
  - *Groups-specific evidence doesn't exist yet (not launched). To collect from launch: the share of groups fully paid before booking, time to a fully paid group, and organiser testimonials with a number in them.*

### Audience 2: The invited friend (asked to join and pay; may never have used Roamly)
- **Value prop:** Say yes to the plan and pay just your part, in a couple of taps. No owing the organiser, no wondering what's happening on the day.
- **Capabilities:**
  - The invite link opens the experience and the shared plan.
  - Pay your own share directly, without paying the organiser back afterwards.
  - See the same plan as everyone else: when, where, what.
  - Book the next experience yourself after the celebration.
- **Evidence:**
  - Hosts are vetted local experts, consistently rated among the best in the category.
  - Travellers come back to Roamly at three times the industry repeat rate.
  - *Gap: nothing yet answers what a first-time payer worries about most: is the payment safe, and what happens if the group falls through? Refund and drop-out rules for split payments are still undecided.*


## 4. Copy across three surfaces

- **Surface 1 (In-app notification):** "Booking for the whole group? Send one link and everyone pays their own share. You don't cover anyone." *Leads with the value prop (no more paying for everyone), at the moment an existing traveller books for more than one person.*
- **Surface 2 (Email subject line):** "Planning the birthday? This time, you don't pay for everyone." *Leads with the value prop, named for a specific occasion so the right organiser recognises themselves in the inbox.*
- **Surface 3 (Social post):** "The friend who books the hen weekend shouldn't also be the one chasing eight people for money. Pick a local experience with a host who's ready for groups, send one link, and everyone pays their own share. Travellers book Roamly again at three times the industry rate. Roamly Groups, for the trips you plan around what you'll do together." *Leads with the From (the organiser's pain), adds capabilities and closes on evidence: a social post has room for all three layers, and the community shares the pain before it shares a product.*
